Not all smart home gadgets earn their place. For every genuinely useful device, there are five that overpromise and underdeliver — adding complexity without meaningfully improving your daily life. We spent three months testing the most popular smart home products to separate the keepers from the clutter.

## Our Testing Criteria

We evaluated each device against four questions:
1. Does it solve a real, recurring problem?
2. Does it work reliably without constant troubleshooting?
3. Does it integrate with other devices in a useful way?
4. Would we pay for it with our own money?

## The Keepers

### Smart Speaker / Voice Assistant
The hub of any smart home. After testing all three major platforms, we recommend picking the ecosystem that matches your phone — Apple HomePod for iPhone users, Google Nest for Android users. Both deliver reliable voice control and serve as the command center for other devices.

Key use cases: timers, weather, music, morning news briefings, and controlling other smart devices.

### Smart Thermostat
A smart thermostat pays for itself. Our testing showed energy savings of around 10–15% on heating and cooling — not because of any magic algorithm, but because the auto-schedule and away-detection features prevent heating or cooling an empty house. Installation is straightforward if you have a standard HVAC system.

### Robot Vacuum with LiDAR
The difference between a random-pattern robot vacuum and one with LiDAR navigation is night and day. LiDAR models map your space precisely, clean in orderly rows, and let you set no-go zones from your phone. After three months, it's genuinely reduced our weekly cleaning time.

### Smart Plugs
The simplest and cheapest entry point. Smart plugs let you schedule lamps, fans, and coffee makers, control them remotely, and monitor energy usage. They're the unsung heroes of a functional smart home — not flashy, but consistently useful.

## What We'd Skip

- **Smart fridge:** A tablet glued to a fridge door does not justify the premium.
- **Smart blinds:** Expensive, finicky to install, and the motors can be noisy.
- **WiFi-connected air fryers:** Your air fryer doesn't need an app.

## The Golden Rule

A smart home should fade into the background. If you're spending more time managing devices than they're saving you, something's wrong. Start with a smart speaker, a thermostat, and a couple of plugs — and only add more when you have a clear use case.
